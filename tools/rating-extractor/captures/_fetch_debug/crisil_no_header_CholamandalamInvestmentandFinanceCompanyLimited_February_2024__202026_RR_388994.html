Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its 'Crisil A1+' rating on the commercial paper of Cholamandalam Investment and Finance Company Ltd (Chola Finance).

 

The rating continues to factor in the established market position of the company - especially in the vehicle financing segment, stability in its profitability despite nominal inch up in delinquencies and strong association with the Murugappa group. These strengths are partially offset by moderate gearing and the ability to maintain adequate buffer in capitalisation metrics alongside growth, remaining a monitorable.

 

Chola Finance remains a leading player in the Indian non-banking financial company (NBFC) sector with a sizable market share in the vehicle financing segment. On December 31, 2025, the company’s AUM stood at Rs 2,14,923 crore marking a 3-year compounded annual growth rate of ~30%.

 

Over the years, the company has diversified into, and garnered moderate market presence in, non-vehicle asset classes like loan against property (LAP), home loans, micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSME) lending, consumer finance and personal loans. As the newer portfolios (Small and Medium Enterprise loan [SME], Consumer and Small Enterprise Loan [CSEL], Secured Business and Personal Loan [SBPL] and Others) have seasoned over time and their share in the overall AUM has increased, steady state asset quality has also evolved. Gross stage III (GS III) has risen marginally to 3.4% as on December 31, 2025, from 2.8% on March 31, 2025 – largely driven by segments such as Vehicle finance, SBPL, and SME. Nonetheless, profitability remains steady with return on managed assets (RoMA) of 2.4% for fiscal 2025 and 2.2% for the first nine months of fiscal 2025.

 

Networth stood at Rs 27,577 crore on December 31, 2025 whereas on the same date - Tier I and overall capital adequacy ratio (CAR) was 14.2% and 19.2% as compared to 14.4% and 19.8%, nine months ago. Gearing, however, remained moderate and at 7.2 times on December 31, 2025 slightly lower than 7.4 times on March 31, 2025. In fiscal 2026, Rs 1370 crore of compulsorily convertible debentures (CCDs) have been converted to equity capital that has added to the capitalisation buffers and another Rs 630 crore are to be converted in early fiscal 2027 which will support further.

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has assessed the standalone credit risk profile of Chola Finance.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Established market position, especially in the vehicle financing segment

Chola Finance has retained its strong market position within the NBFC sector, catalysed by sustained growth in the vehicle financing portfolio and gradual diversification into newer business segments such as consumer and micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSME) loans [called Consumer and Small Enterprise Loan (CSEL)], secured business and personal loan (SBPL) and SME finance.

 

Overall disbursements grew by 14% in fiscal 2025 and 6% (year-on-year) during the nine months ended December 31, 2025; active exit from sub-par fintech partnerships, curtailed disbursements in consumer loans, exit from supply chain financing within SMEs, and limited deployment towards Home loan portfolio due to operational issues - have led to lower disbursements in the first nine months of fiscal 2026. Some effect of this was partly offset by disbursements in vehicle and LAP segments, where momentum remained steady. Consequently, total AUM grew by 21% (year-on-year) to Rs 214,922 crore as on December 31, 2025 from Rs 188,157 crore as on March 31, 2025 (Rs 148,167 crore as on March 31, 2024).

 

As one of the largest vehicle financiers in the country, the company has a sizable market share in the new and used vehicle financing segment, which accounts for half of its overall AUM. As on December 31, 2025, the vehicle finance AUM stood at Rs 115,881 crore comprising commercial vehicles and Construction equipment (CV+CE; 26%), cars and utility vehicle (19%), two-/three-wheelers (5%), and tractors (4%). Furthermore 72% of the vehicle-financing AUM comprised new vehicles. Over the years, the company has developed expertise in catering to credit needs of small and medium-fleet transport operators and first-time users, which enables them to customize credit offerings while appropriately factoring in the inherent risks.

 

The company has also gained a sizeable presence in the loan against property (LAP) business, reflected in an AUM base of Rs 49,711 crore (23% of overall AUM) as on December 31, 2025 which marks a year-on-year growth of 31%. Within the housing loan segment as well, the company has registered a year-on-year growth of 27% to achieve an AUM of Rs 21,867 crore (10% of overall AUM) as on December 31, 2025. This diversification, supported by healthy demand prospects and branch expansion, imparts diversity to the overall portfolio of the company which insulates the business from cyclical downturn in the vehicle industry.  The company has also been growing its portfolio in the newer segments especially post the recent conscious exits – like Consumer & Small Enterprise Loan (CSEL), Secured Business and Personal Loan (SBPL) and Small and Medium Enterprise Loan (SME) finance which were launched in fiscal 2022 – and cumulatively account for 13 % of the overall AUM as on December 31, 2025, and have clocked a year-on-year growth of 17%.

 

Over the medium to long term, traditional segments like vehicle finance will continue to account for the majority share in overall AUM with CV and UV financing remaining the flagship product. However, gradual expansion into newer business segments like LAP, home loans and SBPL shall result in higher portfolio diversity over time.

 

Stable earnings profile despite nominal inch up in delinquencies

Overall earnings profile has remained comfortable with a RoMA of 2.2% for 9M 2025 as against 2.4% for full fiscal 2025 and 2.5% for fiscal 2024. For 9M 2025, profit after tax (PAT) was Rs. 3,579 crore on total net income (net of interest expense) of Rs. 12,281 crore as against a PAT of Rs. 2,992 crore and a total net income of Rs. 9,812 crore, respectively, for the corresponding period of the previous fiscal. This has been supported by Net Interest Margin (NIMs) and Operating expenses (as % of average of total managed assets) having remained range bound though some moderation was witnessed in credit costs (as a % of total managed assets) that inched up to 1.6% (annualised) for nine months ended December 31, 2025 from 1.4% for full fiscal 2025 and 1.0% for the year before.

 

This increase in credit costs stemmed from a nominal inch up in delinquencies since after the mid of fiscal 2025. On December 31, 2025, Gross Stage (GS) III rose marginally to 3.4% from 2.9% on March 31, 2025, and 2.8% on March 31, 2024, driven by higher slippages across all segments, particularly the newer portfolios.

 

In terms of segmental asset quality, GS III in the vehicle finance portfolio stood at 4.2% as on December 31, 2025, as compared to 3.5% on March 31, 2025 and 3.0%, a year prior. In the LAP portfolio, GS III was 2.4% on December 31, 2025 as compared to 2.0% as on March 31, 2025, and has further scope of improvement. In the SBPL portfolio, which is a relatively newer business segment, delinquencies have remained slightly elevated as most of the portfolio is non-SARFAESI backed and requires 2-3 years for recoveries. The company has actively exited the fintech partnerships and restricted disbursements in CSEL portfolio, hence, the asset quality going forward will remain insulated from unsecured exposures.

 

Delinquencies within the new business segments have been controlled through active calls taken for certain higher risk segments. However, the ability of the company to profitably scale new businesses while maintaining sound asset quality in traditional segments like vehicle, will remain a key monitorable going forward.

 

Continued benefit of association with the Murugappa group

Chola Finance continues to benefit from the financial and management support from the Murugappa group, which holds a substantial equity stake of 49.72% in the company as on December 31, 2025 - through Cholamandalam Financial Holdings Ltd (44.18%) and other group entities. Over the years, the overall credit risk profile of the company has strengthened and in addition to it, the group has a track record of extending timely funding support to Chola Finance during weak liquidity conditions or other adverse macro developments. In fiscal 2020, the group infused Rs 300 crore in Chola Finance, thereby augmenting its capital position. Chola Finance remains a critical component of the Murugappa group ecosystem, witnessed by its high contribution to the group’s consolidated revenues and overall market capitalisation. The group is likely to maintain its majority stake in the company and shall provide need-based financial and managerial support.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Moderate leverage level, ability to maintain adequate buffer in capitalisation metrics alongside growth remains critical

Overall capital-to-risk weighted assets ratio (CRAR) and Tier-1 CAR stood at 19.2% and 14.2%, as on December 31, 2025, as compared to 19.8% and 14.4%, respectively, as on March 31, 2025. Earlier in fiscal 2024, the company raised Rs 2,000 crore through equity infusion and another Rs 2,000 crore through CCDs via qualified institutional placements.

 

Chola Finance has had an elevated adjusted gearing level of >6.5 times over the years; as on March 31, 2025, adjusted gearing was at 7.4 times which improved slightly to 7.2 times on December 31, 2025, due to conversion of CCDs worth Rs 307 crore during the period and steady internal accruals, furthermore Rs 1063 crore has been converted in January 2026. While the company is an active issuer in the capital markets and has demonstrated track record for raising capital from there, its ability to maintain adequate buffer in reported capitalisation metrics remains critical.

Liquidity Strong

The company had liquid cushion aggregating to Rs 21,808 crore in the form of cash and equivalent (Rs 1,830 crore), Liquid investments (Rs 16,501 crore) and unutilized bank lines (Rs 3,478 crore) as on December 31, 2025. The expected contractual inflows for the succeeding 2 months (January to February 2026) amount to Rs 10,942 crore. Against this, for the same 2-month period, the company had a debt obligation of Rs 13,416 crore.

Environment, social and governance (ESG) profile

Crisil Ratings believes the environment, social and governance (ESG) profile of Chola Finance supports its already strong credit risk profile.

 

The ESG profile for financial sector entities typically factors in governance as a key differentiator. The sector has reasonable social impact because of its substantial employee and customer base, and it can play a key role in promoting financial inclusion. While the sector does not have a direct adverse environmental impact, lending decisions may have a bearing on environment.

 

Chola Finance has maintained strong focus on strengthening various aspects of its ESG profile.

 

Key ESG highlights:

  • CIFCL’s has integrated environmental consciousness into its business responsibility framework, focusing on reducing its carbon footprint through a responsible process. In this light, its Scope 1 and 2 emissions intensity (in terms of physical output) fell to 0.31 in 2024-25 from 0.35 in the previous year.
  • In fiscal 2025, the company emphasized the use of sensor-based lighting and renewable energy at its corporate headquarters. The energy consumption intensity stands at ~0.5 MWh per employee with a nominal share of energy being consumed from renewable sources (~0.2% of the total energy mix)
  • Additionally, CIFCL is ambitious on achieving its targets on increasing EV loan disbursements by 5% y-o-y between fiscal 2023 and fiscal 2025, attaining green building status for corporate office, replacing CFL lighting with LED systems at all operations, and reducing water consumption across the company operations through efficient water flow solutions
  • Further, CIFCL’s loan book is majorly dominated by vehicle financing with a significant share of financing pre-owned vehicles which have detrimental effects on the environment. However, the other half of the loan book is diversified into housing loans, small and medium enterprises (SME) loans, consumer finance, loan against property (LAP), and secured business and personal loans, which has relatively moderate environmental impacts while positively aiding societal developments
  • As of March 31, 2025, the share of women employees in the total workforce stood at ~3% which is consistent from previous three years. Also, the attrition rate of employees fell by ~6 percentage points from fiscal 2024 standing at ~27% in fiscal 2025
  • CIFCL operates one of the most extensive rural networks in India with ~1,613 branches with 92% of locations in Tier III and Tier IV towns
  • Furthermore, the CSR initiatives have impacted over 3.5 million beneficiaries in last five years as of fiscal 2025 with major projects undertaken by the company focusing on the areas such as health, water & sanitation, education, arts & culture, sports, rural development, environmental sustainability, research & development, and road safety

 

The company’s governance structure is characterized by high share of independent directors (~63%), with segregation in chairperson and managing director positions, presence of a dedicated investor grievance redressal.

 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. The commitment of Chola Finance to ESG principles will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence, given the sizeable share of its market borrowings in overall debt and access to both domestic and foreign capital markets.

Rating sensitivity factors

Downward factors

  • Significant and sustained deterioration in asset quality with GNPA increasing to, and remaining beyond 5%, leading to weakening in profitability
  • Inability to maintain capitalisation metrics at healthy levels alongside scale up in business

About the Company

Part of the Chennai-based Murugappa group, Chola Finance was incorporated in 1978. The company provides vehicle financing and LAP as well as home loans, MSME and agricultural loans. It has ventured into new businesses in the consumer and MSME ecosystems, namely CSEL, SBPL and SME finance in the second half of fiscal 2022. It had 1,757 branches across 26 states and 7 union territories in India, with 92% presence across tier III to tier VI cities, as on December 31, 2025.

 

Between April 2005 and March 2010, the company operated as a joint venture between DBS Bank and the Murugappa group. In March 2010, DBS Bank sold its 37.5% equity stake to the Murugappa group. Chola Finance exited the unsecured personal loan segment in October 2008 and subsequently from the asset management business. The Murugappa group holds 49.72% equity stake in Chola Finance, of which 44.18% is held by Cholamandalam Financial Holdings Ltd, a group company.

 

Chola Finance has two subsidiaries: Cholamandalam Securities Ltd and Cholamandalam Home Finance Ltd, a joint venture with Payswiff Technologies Pvt Ltd and three associates: White Data Systems India Pvt Ltd, Vishvakarma Payments Pvt Ltd and Paytail Commerce Pvt Ltd.
